Automate the rest

The work that eats your week, done without you.

Every small business runs on a set of small manual steps nobody wrote down. Copy the name into the calendar. Text the crew the address. Send the reminder. Update the sheet. None of them take long. Together they take your evenings. We find them and take them off your plate.

The problem

It is not one big time sink. It is forty small ones.

The tasks below are the ones we find in almost every business we look at.

The same data, typed three times

A customer comes in by phone, gets written on a pad, typed into the calendar, then typed again into the invoice. Three chances to make a typo and twenty minutes gone.

Manual scheduling ping-pong

Four texts back and forth to land on Tuesday at two. Then a reschedule, and four more. This is the single most common time sink we find.

Reminders that depend on memory

Appointment reminders, deposit reminders, annual service reminders — all real revenue, all dependent on somebody remembering to send them on a busy day.

Handoffs that fall through

Sales books it, but the crew never got the notes. Office says it is scheduled, the tech says it is not. Every handoff without a system is a dropped ball waiting to happen.

How it works

How we find and remove it.

1

We shadow your actual week

A short working session where you walk us through a normal Tuesday — every step from first contact to paid invoice. We write down what you do and how long it takes.

2

Rank by hours, not by novelty

We put a number on each task: how often, how long, how much it costs when it goes wrong. Then we start with the biggest one, not the most interesting one.

3

Build and run it alongside you

The automation goes live in parallel with your current process for a week, so you can see it working before you trust it. Nothing gets switched off until it has proven itself.

4

Hand it over, then find the next one

You get documentation and the ability to change it yourself. Then we look at the next task on the list.

Questions

The things owners actually ask.

Usually the opposite. It takes the repetitive part off them so they can do the work that actually needs a human — talking to customers, handling the odd situations, chasing what matters. Most owners we work with were about to hire and did not need to.

Whatever you have is a starting point. We build most of this in GoHighLevel, and it connects to the calendars, accounting software and field tools you already use. If you are running the whole business on a paper pad and a group text, that is fine too — we have started there before.

Most are, in the details. That is why we start by watching how you actually work rather than dropping in a template. The point is to automate your process, not to make you adopt someone else's.

Before we start, we write down what the task costs you now — hours per week, or dollars. After it is live we measure the same thing. If the number did not move, we say so and change the approach.
