The problem

Why the good ones get away.

Four things go wrong in almost every small business hiring process.

Applications sit for days

Resumes land in an inbox during your busiest week. By the time you get to them on Sunday night, the two people worth calling have already accepted somewhere else.

Screening by reading resumes

Fifty applications, most unqualified, and no fast way to tell. So you either read all of them badly or you read none of them.

Scheduling eats the week

Phone tag to set an interview, then a no-show, then rescheduling. Most small businesses lose more time to interview logistics than to interviews.

Silence between steps

A candidate who hears nothing for a week assumes they did not get it and takes another offer. Most of this is not rudeness — it is a busy owner with no system.

How it works

What the automated version looks like.

1

Application triggers instant contact

Within minutes of applying, the candidate gets a text acknowledging it and asking the first screening questions. You have engaged them before your competitor has opened their inbox.

2

Screening happens automatically

Knockout questions on license, experience, availability, drug test, clean driving record — whatever actually matters for the role. Unqualified applicants get a polite, prompt no.

3

Qualified candidates self-schedule

A link to your real interview availability. They pick a slot, it lands on your calendar, and reminders go out before it so they show up.

4

Nobody goes silent

Automatic updates at every stage, right through to offer and first day. Candidates stay warm, and the ones you pass on leave with a decent impression.
