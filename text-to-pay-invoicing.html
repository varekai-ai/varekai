Get paid before you leave the driveway.

The gap between finishing the work and getting the money is where small businesses bleed. Not from customers who refuse to pay — from customers who mean to, and then do not, because paying you takes effort. Text-to-pay removes the effort.

The problem

Every day an invoice sits unpaid, it gets harder to collect.

Collection rates drop sharply after the first week. Here is what causes the delay in the first place.

Invoices land in email and die there

Email gets opened at maybe a fifth the rate of a text. An emailed invoice competes with everything else in an inbox and usually loses.

You are the one doing the chasing

Somebody has to remember who has not paid, then make an awkward phone call about it. Most owners put that off, which is exactly how ninety days happen.

Paying you is inconvenient

Mail a check, or call during business hours to read a card number out loud. Every extra step is another reason to deal with it later.

Nothing reconciles automatically

Payments come in through three different channels and someone hand-matches them to jobs at the end of the month, usually getting at least one wrong.

How it works

How it works in practice.

1

Job closes, invoice fires

When you mark the job complete, the invoice generates and a payment link goes out by text within a minute — while you are still on site and the work is fresh.

2

They tap and pay

The link opens to a payment page with the amount already filled in. Card or bank, on their phone, in about twenty seconds. No app, no account, no login.

3

Reminders run themselves

If it is unpaid, automatic reminders go out on a schedule you set — day three, day seven, day fourteen — with the same link, in a tone you approve. You never make the awkward call.

4

It reconciles itself

Payment posts against the job, the customer record updates, receipts go out automatically, and your accounting stays current without a month-end scramble.
