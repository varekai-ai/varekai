Missed-call text-back

Every missed call gets a text back before they call someone else.

The average small business misses roughly one in four inbound calls. Most of those callers do not leave a voicemail — they dial the next number on the list. This closes that window automatically, day or night, for businesses around Fort Worth and Weatherford.

The problem

A missed call is not a missed message. It is a job someone else booked.

You are on a ladder, under a sink, or with a customer. The phone rings and you cannot get to it. By the time you call back that evening, they have already had someone out to quote the work.

They text back

People who will not leave a voicemail will absolutely answer a text. The medium is the reason it works.

Nights and weekends

The calls you miss most are the ones outside business hours, which are also the ones with the most urgency behind them.

You already paid for it

That caller found you through your ads, your map listing or a referral. Losing them means buying another one to replace them.

It compounds

Two missed calls a week at your average ticket is a number worth working out. The calculator on the home page does it for you.

How it works

Four steps, and none of them are yours.

1

The call goes unanswered

Busy, after hours, or nobody near the phone. Your number and how you answer it do not change.

2

A text goes out in seconds

In your voice, naming your business and what you do. Not a generic auto-reply.

3

The conversation continues

It answers the usual questions, finds out what the job is, and offers times from your calendar.

4

You get a booked job

Or a thread you can pick up from your phone whenever you get down off the ladder.
